# Almanakk — conventions, reconsidered for the native iPhone app

Written 09.10.2026 on Alan's word: "please rethink all other conventions". Every
convention the almanac reads out of plain text is listed here, with what the native app
should do with it. **Decided** means Alan has said yes. Everything else is a proposal
waiting for his word.

The rule behind every proposal: Google Calendar stays the single source of truth, and
anything stored there must read sensibly in every other client (Apple Calendar, Google,
colleagues who subscribe). The app may offer a nicer way to write a mark. It must never
invent a mark that only the app understands.

| # | Convention today | Native app | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pencilled: a lone `P` first in the notes, plus `?` in the title by habit | One mark, the `?` ending the title. A Blyant switch in the app; confirming is one tap that removes the `?`. Old P notes are listed and converted only on Alan's yes. | **Decided 09.10**; built in web bygg 20261009f |
| 2 | `tbc` / `HOLD` in a title = tentative | `tbc` and `HOLD` are the tour robot's words: read, never written by the app. Alan's own tentative things use the `?` (1), including his own moves. | **Decided 09.10** |
| 3 | A move is an event titled `-Roma` / `→ Roma`, `tbc` when planned, an optional clock time to order it after a flight | Keep the event: it is how a subscriber sees the move. The app writes it from a "Reise til…" picker (place, date, time, Blyant), so nobody types the arrow syntax. | **Decided 09.10** |
| 4 | Flights found by parsing titles (dash legs, IATA codes, booking references, "fly/flight") | Port the parser with its test tables, unchanged. Long term, the parked Gmail importer writes flights in one fixed format to its own calendar, and the parser shrinks to reading that format. | **Decided 09.10** |
| 5 | Mail-scraped flights (`fromGmail`) never move the city | Keep. | Keep |

| 16 | Time zones | TIMES FOLLOW THE CITY HE IS IN THAT DAY (Alan, 09–10.10: "it should predict I will be in Rome for that dinner"; "dinner at 19, not a weird 13:00 because I am in Oslo"). Reading: every timed event shows at the local time of that day's city (the city column: flights, moves, tour legs), so a week in Asia reads in Asian time from anywhere; a call shows at the time where he takes it ("the call happens at the time of the place I am"), and the day sheet also names the event's own zone when it differs. Exception, his yes: a FLIGHT keeps its ticket time. Writing: a new event defaults to that day's city zone; Google's zone field in the form overrides. Unknown city: the event's own zone, as today. Needs city → zone: the airport dataset carries a zone per airport; towns without one get theirs by hand, like EXTRA_PLACES. | **Decided 10.10** |
| 17 | Flights are written in one form | The skill `add-flight` (claude.ai, so every Claude has it): title `Flight AAA-BBB` (route only, never a flight number or comma), location = departure airport and terminal, start/end in departure/arrival zones, notes led by `REF · SURNAME/NAME` exactly as booked, then airline + number, terminals, boarding, extras, and the Apple Mail link. 09.10's "Flight AAA-BBB, Airline XX 123" moved nothing; the almanac now also reads a comma as the end of a route (bygg 20261010a). | **Decided 10.10** |
| 18 | A flight in the day sheet | Route and local times; the booking reference large, a tap copies it; "Sjekk inn" copies the reference and opens the airline's check-in page (its app when installed, by iOS's own link handling) from a short airline → check-in address list; airline, number, terminals, boarding; "✉ Åpne e-posten". | 19 | The tour band's name and city | The NAME and CITY hang on the free row ABOVE the band, so every tour day keeps its word (Travel, Get in, Work, the show numbers). That row keeps its own ground (a Sunday stays grey: it is not a tour day); the band's left rule climbs into it, with no tint and no underline; the words are 11pt in BLACK INK, name bold and city plain, never shrunk (his eyesight; the tour's brown read as red beside the shows), running on to the right over an empty cell and, when something stands there, cutting the city before the name. (Alan, 10.10, three rounds: a tinted cap made the 25th look like a tour day; an underline ran under only half the words.) A heading on a Monday sends the week number to Tuesday and the weekly city to Wednesday, the same step-down a Monday holiday makes. Fallback when the row above is taken (a leg on the 1st, or straight after another): name and city on the first row; a leg that only CONTINUES from last month shows its name alone on the 1st, since his city column says where he is. Built in the native month. | **Decided 10.10** |
| 20 | No emoji | A calendar entry is plain words: no emoji, symbol or decoration in title, notes or location, from any Claude (rule in ClaudeCode/CLAUDE.md, Desk/CLAUDE.md, the nightly task; Alan to add it to his claude.ai profile). | 21 | Flight routes and joined journeys | Every city stays on the line: "OSL-PAR-HKG" reads "Oslo → Paris → Hong Kong"; two flights the same day that meet read as one journey, "Oslo → Kairo → Amsterdam", at the first one's time. | 23 | Moon phases | New moon, first and last quarter, full moon: a small mark by the weekday letter in the month, in the week's day head, and named in the day sheet. (Alan, 11.10: "moon phases we need!") | **Decided 11.10**, native |

No standing button (Alan, 11.10: "should regret sit so long? … I like shake undo … and the three fingers"). | **Decided 11.10**, native |

## Order for the native build

The month view comes first: rows, tour column, day line, colours, pencil grey. It is
followed by the day sheet with editing, the week view, and the year. Each convention
above lands with the view that first needs it.
